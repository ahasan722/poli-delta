# Story candidates: five theses the evidence could carry

Each thesis lists the 3-4 facts that would do the work, then an honest grading. "Strong" = peer-reviewed or official primary, recent. "Weak" = press, political statement, undated, or internally inconsistent.

---

## 1. The delta is not drowning, it is being starved: the real threat is halved sediment, not rising sea alone

**Carrying facts**
- GBM sediment flux is ~500 Mt/yr on BWDB data, roughly half the 1 Gt/yr still assumed in planning, and falling 4-10 Mt/yr every year (Rahman et al. 2018).
- Historic load was ~1 Gt/yr and early Holocene ~2.5 Gt/yr; about a third of what arrives is lost to the Swatch of No Ground canyon (Kuehl et al.).
- Where sediment still reaches the surface, land keeps up: Sundarbans platforms gain ~1.8 cm per wet season against effective SLR of 0.7-1.7 cm/yr (Hale et al. 2019); the Meghna estuary has netted +17 to +36 km2/yr for a century (Sarker et al.).
- Dam/river-linking scenarios cut Ganges delta supply by 88% (Dunn et al., cited in Rahman 2018) and delta aggradation from 0.36 to 0.25 cm/yr (Hale et al.).

**Strength**: Strong. All four pillars are peer-reviewed. Caveat: Rahman 2018 itself gives 500 vs 750 Mt/yr in different sections; quote the 500 Mt/yr abstract figure and say "about half". Weak link: the 88% decline is a model scenario, not an observation.

## 2. Bangladesh built walls against the sea and the land inside them sank: polders lost 1-1.5 m and now sit below high tide

**Carrying facts**
- Polder interiors have lost 1.0-1.5 m of elevation relative to the adjacent Sundarbans since the 1960s (Auerbach et al. 2015, Nature Climate Change); 139 polders cover >1.2 Mha (Water History 2022).
- Shallow subsidence is 5 ± 1 mm/yr inside Polder 32 vs 8.7-9 mm/yr in mangroves, but mangroves also receive sediment and polders do not (Frontiers 2024 review).
- Aila 2009 (3 m surge, ~1,000 km of embankment damaged) flooded Polder 32 for two years and left 200,000 homeless a year later (Wikipedia/Auerbach).
- Tidal River Management reverses it where tried: Beel Bhaina raised 0.8 m with 6.48 Mm3 of silt in four years; East Beel Khuksia 1.2 m; but when TRM stopped at Beel Kedaria the Hari river bed rose 4-6 m (IntechOpen TRM chapter).

**Strength**: Strong on the physics (Auerbach, Frontiers 2024, TRM figures from an engineering review). Weak: the "126 islands, 8,000 km2, 20 million people" framing comes from a university press release; the polder count (108 + 31) is reconstructed, and embankment length is given as both 5,000 and 8,000 km in the same source. Note that the CEP was US-designed (IECO/USAID), not Dutch; the Dutch role is the later Blue Gold rehabilitation (22 polders, 119,000 ha, 2013-2021).

## 3. Deaths fell 10,000-fold while exposure rose: shelters and volunteers, not milder storms, explain the cyclone story

**Carrying facts**
- Deaths: Bhola 1970 300,000-500,000 (46% of Tazumuddin's population); Gorky 1991 138,866; Sidr 2007 3,447; Amphan 2020 26; Remal 2024 about 10 (Wikipedia infoboxes citing IMD/government; GFDRR JDLNA for Sidr; TBS/New Humanitarian for Amphan).
- Winds did not weaken: 1991 235 km/h (IMD) vs Sidr 215 km/h vs Mocha 2023 180-190 km/h at landfall; surges 10 m (1970), 6.1 m (1991), >5 m (2007).
- Shelters: 44 in 1970, 512 in 1991, >14,000 today with 2.4 million capacity; CPP has 76,000 volunteers; 2.1 million evacuated for Bulbul, 2.4 million for Amphan (New Humanitarian, TBS, IFRC DREF).
- IDMC counts ~11.3 million pre-emptive cyclone evacuations 2008-2024 as life-saving movements rather than displacement.

**Strength**: The death series is strong (official tolls, consistently reported). The wind comparison is only indicative because IMD 3-min and JTWC 1-min figures are mixed across pages; pick one agency per storm. Shelter counts by year are weak-to-medium (press and NGO, no official annual series found; World Bank MDSP gives 3,268 multipurpose shelters in 2015, which conflicts with the 14,000 figure unless the latter includes schools and temporary sites). Capacity 2.4 million vs coastal population 35 million is a good tension figure but comes from IFRC via a newspaper.

## 4. The quiet hazard beats the loud ones: riverbank erosion and salinity move more people, permanently, than any cyclone

**Carrying facts**
- Jamuna, Padma and Ganges have eroded 158,353 ha (1,585 km2) in five decades, Jamuna alone 94,220 ha; CEGIS put the rate at ~6,000 ha/yr and 50,000 people made homeless per year in 2009 (CEGIS via press).
- IOM's first nationwide stock count (Dec 2025) found 4.96 million disaster IDPs, with Sirajganj and Bhola (erosion districts) among the four districts holding a quarter of them; 85% are still in rural unions (UN/IOM press release).
- Saline soil rose from 0.83 Mha (1973) to 1.05 Mha (2009), and tides reach 180-200 km inland in the dry season; the World Bank projects a 26% median salinity rise by 2050 and 15.6% rice-yield loss in nine upazilas (Salehin 2018, Banglapedia, World Bank 2015).
- Post-Farakka, March flow at Hardinge Bridge fell from ~1,980 cumec to 261 cumec in 1993, and the Gorai chokes by January (Banglapedia).

**Strength**: Erosion totals are official CEGIS numbers but reach us through press; the 2025 erosion (1,034 ha) is well below the 2009-era 6,000 ha/yr, so be careful not to imply a constant rate. The IOM count is new and strong. The salinity series is strong (SRDI via a peer-reviewed chapter). Farakka flow figures are from Banglapedia (encyclopaedic, dated) and the "halved" claim lacks a cited time series; the Gorai dry-season flow is described, not quantified.

## 5. Elevation data rewrote the risk map: Bangladesh's flood-exposed population is 5-10 times higher than SRTM said, and the land itself is sinking at 3-7 mm/yr

**Carrying facts**
- CoastalDEM puts 42 million Bangladeshis on land below the annual flood line by 2050 (moderate emissions) versus 5 million on SRTM; 50 million vs 6 million below the 2100 high-tide line under high emissions (Kulp & Strauss 2019, Nature Communications).
- Delta water levels rose 3 mm/yr 1968-2012 (vs 2 mm/yr global) across 101 gauges, with subsidence contributing 1-7 mm/yr; relative rise of 85-140 cm by 2100, double the IPCC figure that ignored subsidence (Becker et al. 2020, PNAS).
- Continuous GNSS gives 3-7 mm/yr subsidence, RSET 9-10 mm/yr in young deposits, only 2-3 mm/yr from deep processes; Brown & Nicholls' 205 records have a median of 2.9 mm/yr but a range to 43.8 (Steckler 2022, Frontiers 2024, Brown & Nicholls 2015).
- Dhaka's water table has dropped from 40 m (2001) to 109 m (2024), ~3 m/yr, with 74% of supply from 1,351 deep tube wells (Dhaka WASA via Dhaka Tribune).

**Strength**: Strongest thesis on paper: three peer-reviewed pillars (Kulp & Strauss, Becker, the subsidence syntheses). Weak points: the CoastalDEM figures come via the Climate Central press release, not the paper text (PMC was captcha-blocked); national shares of land below 1/5/10 m were not found in a citable source and should be computed from a DEM rather than quoted; the Dhaka groundwater numbers are official but reported in press, and no Dhaka subsidence rate in mm/yr was retrievable (Higgins 2014 InSAR paper blocked).

## Cross-cutting cautions
- Flood inundation percentages differ by method: BUET satellite series (1988 61%, 1998 68%, 2004 38%, 2007 42%, 2017 42%, 2020 37%) vs Wikipedia table (1988 52%, 1998 67%) vs NAWG 2020 24%. Use one series and label it.
- Cyclone winds: always state IMD (3-min) or JTWC (1-min); the pages mix them.
- Tide-gauge trends at individual Bangladesh stations (Khepupara 18.66 mm/yr etc.) are from a weak source with typos and no periods; use Becker 2020's 3 mm/yr delta average and the CEGIS regional ranges instead.
- The "6 million char dwellers" and "17% of Bangladesh under water by 2050" figures are widely repeated but untraceable to data; both are flagged in facts.csv.
- 2025-2026 cyclones: searches found only a May 2025 deep depression (4 Bangladesh deaths) and no 2026 landfall as of Oct 2026; confirm with BMD before stating "no major cyclone".
