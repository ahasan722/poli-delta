# Sources: Ganges-Brahmaputra-Meghna delta evidence base

Annotated list of the 25 most useful documents found (Oct 2026). Grade: A = peer-reviewed or official primary; B = official/agency secondary or reputable NGO; C = press reporting of an official figure.

## Physical system: sediment, subsidence, elevation

1. **Rahman et al. 2018, "Recent sediment flux to the Ganges-Brahmaputra-Meghna delta system", Sci Total Environ** (A)
   https://generic.wordpress.soton.ac.uk/deccma/wp-content/uploads/sites/181/2017/02/Rahman-et-al.-2018.pdf
   The best single source on the sediment budget: BWDB 1960-2008 data, GBM flux ~500 Mt/yr (not the 1 Gt/yr planning assumption), Ganges 150-590 and Brahmaputra 135-615 Mt/yr, declining 4-10 Mt/yr per year, projections to 2030.

2. **Kuehl, Allison, Goodbred, Kudrass, "The Ganges-Brahmaputra Delta" (SEPM special publication)** (A)
   https://www.vims.edu/people/kuehl_sa/pubs/SEPMG-BDelta.pdf
   Delta geometry (110,000 km2 subaerial; 140,000 incl. subaqueous), ~1 Gt/yr historic load, ~30% to subaqueous delta and ~1/3 to Swatch of No Ground, tectonic subsidence 1-4 mm/yr, early Holocene load 2.5x modern.

3. **Wilson & Goodbred 2015, "Construction and maintenance of the GBM delta", Annu Rev Mar Sci 7:67-88** (A)
   https://repository.lsu.edu/geo_pubs/1351
   Framing paper: delta "relatively stable" overall thanks to monsoon dispersal and tides, but sediment-starved portions are losing elevation. Abstract only fetched; full text needed for numbers.

4. **Sarker et al., "Morphological processes in the Meghna Estuary" (IAHS)** (A)
   https://iahs.info/uploads/dms/16307.25-203-218-349-04-Hyderabad-Sarker-ms-edited.pdf
   Period-by-period net accretion of the Meghna estuary (4.6 km2/yr 1776-1943; 36 km2/yr 1943-73; ~17-19 km2/yr 1973-2008), erosion/accretion 860/1370 km2 for 1973-2000, river sediment loads 1994-96.

5. **Sarwar & Woodroffe 2013, "Rates of shoreline change along the coast of Bangladesh", J Coastal Conservation** (A)
   https://ro.uow.edu.au/articles/journal_contribution/Rates_of_shoreline_change_along_the_coast_of_Bangladesh/27741876
   1989-2009 transect analysis: 315 km2 accretion vs 307 km2 erosion (near-zero net), Bhola up to 120 m/yr erosion, Sundarbans margin up to 20 m/yr, Hatiya +50/-65 km2.

6. **Brown & Nicholls 2015, "Subsidence and human influences in mega deltas: the case of the GBM", Sci Total Environ** (A)
   https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/376708
   205 measurements from 24 studies; range -1.1 to 43.8 mm/yr, mean 5.6, median 2.9; highest in Sylhet, Dhaka, Kolkata; many records vague.

7. **Steckler et al. 2022, "Synthesis of the distribution of subsidence of the lower Ganges-Brahmaputra Delta", Earth-Science Reviews 224** (A)
   https://repository.lsu.edu/geo_pubs/2259
   Rates by method and timescale: 1-5 mm/yr (long-term), 4-8 (GNSS/gauges), 9-10 (RSET/strainmeter); explains why shallow young deposits give higher rates.

8. **Frontiers in Earth Science 2024, 10.3389/feart.2024.1354686 (subsidence of the Ganges-Brahmaputra Delta)** (A)
   https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/earth-science/articles/10.3389/feart.2024.1354686/full
   Most recent compilation: continuous GNSS 3-7 mm/yr, Sundarbans RSET 9 ± 1, polder 32 5 ± 1 vs mangrove 8.7 ± 1.2, campaign GNSS 20-25 near coast, InSAR 5-10 (max 18), deep processes only 2-3 mm/yr.

9. **Auerbach et al. 2015, "Flood risk of natural and embanked landscapes on the Ganges-Brahmaputra tidal delta plain", Nature Climate Change** (A, via Vanderbilt news)
   https://news.vanderbilt.edu/2015/01/05/flood-control-efforts-in-bangladesh-exacerbate-flooding-threaten-millions
   Polders have lost 1.0-1.5 m of elevation relative to the Sundarbans since embanking (126 islands, 8,000 km2, ~20 million people); Aila 2009 breach and 2-year inundation of Polder 32.

10. **Hale et al. 2019, "Seasonal variability of forces controlling sedimentation in the Sundarbans", Frontiers Earth Sci 7:211** (A)
    https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/feart.2019.00211/pdf
    Mangrove platform sedimentation 1.8 cm per wet season keeps pace with effective SLR of 0.7 (coast) to 1.7 (Khulna) cm/yr; river-linking would cut delta aggradation 0.36 to 0.25 cm/yr.

## Sea level and elevation exposure

11. **Becker et al. 2020, "Water level changes, subsidence, and sea level rise in the GBM delta", PNAS** (A, via CNRS press release)
    https://www.cnrs.fr/en/press/better-estimate-water-level-rise-ganges-delta
    101 gauges 1968-2012: delta water level +3 mm/yr vs 2 mm/yr global; subsidence 1-7 mm/yr; 85-140 cm relative rise by 2100 (double IPCC).

12. **Kulp & Strauss 2019, "New elevation data triple estimates of global vulnerability to sea-level rise and coastal flooding", Nature Communications** (A, via Climate Central press release)
    https://climatecentral.org/press-release-flooded-future
    CoastalDEM: Bangladesh 42 million on land below annual flood level by 2050 (vs 5 million on SRTM); 50 million below high-tide line by 2100 under high emissions (vs 6 million).

13. **ICCCAD / CEGIS Climate Change Cell study, "Sea level rising by 6-20mm each year"** (B)
    https://icccad.net/?p=1885
    30-year tidal water-level trends: 7-8 mm/yr west, 6-9 central, 11-20 east. Official Bangladesh study but trends include subsidence and gauge problems.

14. **Ahmad 2019, "Bangladesh Coastal Zone Management: Status and Future Trends", J Coastal Zone Management 22:1** (B)
    https://www.walshmedicalmedia.com/open-access/bangladesh-coastal-zone-management-status-and-future-trends-18228.html
    Coastal zone 47,201 km2 (32% of country), 35 million people (29%), 50% of land <8 m, "1 m SLR submerges 18%" (secondary), 718,000 cyclone deaths in 50 years.

## Rivers, erosion, chars

15. **CEGIS riverbank erosion figures (via OdishaBytes, 2026, and BSS/New Nation 2009)** (C, official figures)
    https://odishabytes.com/rivers-swallow-land-five-times-the-size-of-dhaka-in-five-decades/
    https://indiaenvironmentportal.org.in/news-clippings/158-lakh-hectares-devoured-20-lakh-rendered-homeless-in-36-years-by-3-major-rivers
    158,353 ha eroded by Jamuna/Padma/Ganges over five decades (Jamuna 60%), 1,034 ha in 2025, 915 ha forecast 2026; 2009: ~6,000 ha/yr, 50,000 homeless/yr, 2 million since 1973.

16. **Banglapedia: "Riverbank Erosion" and "Char"** (B)
    https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php/Riverbank_Erosion
    https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php/Char
    Jamuna widening 128 m/yr (1973-2000), 70,000 ha floodplain lost, 5,000 ha/yr peak (1984-92); chars 1,722 km2 (1993), char population 631,000 (1993), 75% of chars last <10 years, 729,000 displaced 1981-93.

17. **Daily Star, "New land raises new hope" (CEGIS Meghna estuary analysis)** (C, official figures)
    https://www.thedailystar.net/frontpage/new-land-raises-new-hope-1206460
    1973-2008: 1,642 km2 accreted vs 1,045 km2 eroded (net 597 km2); 40 new chars, 1,643 km2 in 15 years.

## Hazards: floods and cyclones

18. **Daily Star, "Flood engulfs over a third of Bangladesh" (BUET satellite analysis, Prof Saiful Islam)** (C, academic figures)
    https://www.thedailystar.net/frontpage/news/flood-engulfs-over-third-bangladesh-1938449
    Consistent single-method inundation series: 1988 61%, 1998 68%, 2004 38%, 2007 42%, 2017 42%, 2020 ~37%.

19. **ReliefWeb disaster and situation pages (OCHA/NDRCC/IFRC)** (B)
    2020 floods: https://reliefweb.int/disaster/FL-2020-000161-BGD
    2019 floods: https://reliefweb.int/report/bangladesh/bangladesh-monsoon-rains-update-echo-daily-flash-25-july-2019
    2022 Sylhet: https://reliefweb.int/report/bangladesh/bangladesh-floods-final-report-mdrbd028
    Official people-affected counts: 2019 4 million, 2020 5.4 million (24% inundated per NAWG), 2022 7.2 million (94% of Sunamganj submerged).

20. **Wikipedia cyclone pages (1970 Bhola, 1991, Sidr, Aila, Mora, Sitrang, Remal) and 2025 NIO season** (B; infoboxes cite IMD/JTWC/government)
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_Bhola_cyclone ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Bangladesh_cyclone ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclone_Sidr ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclone_Aila ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclone_Remal ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_North_Indian_Ocean_cyclone_season
    Landfall, wind (IMD 3-min and JTWC 1-min), surge, deaths, affected for each storm; 2025 season shows no major Bangladesh landfall.

21. **GFDRR, "Bangladesh 2007 JDLNA assessed US$1.7 billion total damage and losses after Cyclone Sidr"** (A, official assessment)
    https://www.gfdrr.org/en/bangladesh-2007-jdlna-assessed-us-17-billion-total-damage-losses-after-cyclone-sidr
    3,447 deaths, 55,000 injured, 2.3 million households affected, US$1.7 bn damage and losses (housing 50%).

22. **The New Humanitarian, "How Bangladesh is beating the odds on climate disaster deaths" (2021) and TBS "50 years of cyclone preparedness" (2022)** (B)
    https://www.thenewhumanitarian.org/investigation/2021/12/2/how-Bangladesh-is-beating-the-odds-on-climate-disaster-deaths
    https://www.tbsnews.net/thoughts/50-years-cyclone-preparedness-success-saving-lives-not-livelihood-368398
    Shelter count 44 (1970), 512 (1991), >14,000 (2020s, capacity 2.4 million); CPP 76,000 volunteers; death-toll series 500,000 / 138,958 / ~3,500 / 26.

## Salinity, polders, Farakka

23. **Salehin et al. 2018, "Mechanisms and drivers of soil salinity in coastal Bangladesh" (Springer chapter, DECCMA)** (A)
    https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/424691/1/CHAPTER_18_Salehin2018_Chapter_MechanismsAndDriversOfSoilSali.pdf
    SRDI saline area 0.83 Mha (1973) to 1.05 Mha (2009); 1.05 of 2.88 Mha in Khulna+Barisal; brackish tides up to 200 km inland.

24. **Water History 2022, "The Coastal Embankment Project" (10.1007/s12685-022-00301-2) and IntechOpen ch. 74917 on Tidal River Management** (A)
    https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s12685-022-00301-2
    https://www.intechopen.com/chapters/74917
    108 + 31 = 139 polders, >1.2 Mha, ~5,000 km embankments, 1961 start (IECO/USAID, not Dutch); TRM results: Beel Bhaina 0.8 m raised / 6.48 Mm3 silt, East Beel Khuksia 1.2 m, Hari bed rose 4-6 m after TRM stopped.

25. **Banglapedia: "Farakka Barrage" and "Ganges Dependent Area"; World Bank 2015 feature on Dasgupta et al. river/soil salinity** (B)
    https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php/Farakka_Barrage
    https://www.worldbank.org/en/news/feature/2015/02/17/salinity-intrusion-in-changing-climate-scenario-will-hit-coastal-bangladesh-hard
    Hardinge Bridge March flow 1,980 cumec pre-Farakka vs 261 cumec in 1993; diversions >1,130 cumec; Gorai chokes by January; 2050 median soil salinity +26%, rice yield -15.6% in nine upazilas.

## People: displacement and migration (bonus, beyond 25)

- **IDMC 2026 Bangladesh displacement risk short profile** (A) https://api.internal-displacement.org/sites/default/files/2026-02/2026-idmc-bangladesh-displacement-risk-short%20profile.pdf : >21 million displacements 2008-2024, 2.4 million in 2024, modelled 740k/yr riverine and 779k/yr storm surge.
- **IOM / UN Bangladesh press release, 10 Dec 2025** (A) https://bangladesh.un.org/en/306903-iom-launches-first-nationwide-estimate-disaster-induced-internal-displacement-bangladesh : 4,955,527 disaster IDPs, first nationwide stock estimate.
- **World Bank Groundswell note PN2 (2018)** (A) https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/401511522303177090/pdf/124722-BRI-PUBLIC-NEWSERIES-Groundswell-note-PN2.pdf : 13.3 million internal climate migrants by 2050 (pessimistic); 2021 update cited at 19.9 million.
- **Mongabay Oct 2024 (Forest Department tiger census)** (B) https://news.mongabay.com/2024/10/tiger-population-census-in-bangladesh-shows-a-hopeful-upward-trend-in-the-sundarbans : 125 tigers (2024), 114 (2018), 106 (2015), Bangladesh Sundarbans 6,017 km2.
